# 5.8 Expeditions — Deep Space

The **Expedition** is a **PvE** (player versus environment) mission: the player sends a fleet to explore **Deep Space**, at the **16th position** of any system (`[Galaxy : System : 16]`). This zone never holds a planet, it is the unknown. On resolution, the expedition draws a **random outcome**: discovering resources, abandoned ships, **meteorites** or **Solar (SLR)**, all the way to hostile encounters and the black hole that swallows the fleet.

Content **open to everyone**, with no enemy target and no aggressiveness point: an accessible source of income, but never without risk.

### Launching an expedition

| Parameter                   | Rule                                                                      |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 🎯 **Target**               | The 16th position of any system: `[G : S : 16]`.                          |
| ⏸️ **Holding (HOLD)**       | Duration chosen at launch (also serves as a _fleet-save_).                |
| 🛰️ **Simultaneous slots**  | Capped by the **Solarian Mapping** research (see below).              |

**Flow:** Departure → travel → arrival at `[.. : 16]` → **holding** → outcome resolution → fleet return (survivors + loot).

#### Expedition slots

The number of expeditions **in flight at once** depends on the **Solarian Mapping** level: `slots = min(5 ; ⌊√level⌋)`.

| Solarian Mapping | Simultaneous expeditions |
| -------------------- | ------------------------ |
| 1 – 3                | 1                        |
| 4 – 8                | 2                        |
| 9 – 15               | 3                        |
| 16 – 24              | 4                        |
| 25 and +             | **5 (maximum)**          |

### The outcomes

For each expedition, an outcome is drawn randomly according to fixed probabilities:

| Outcome                  | Probability | Effect                               |
| ------------------------ | ----------- | ------------------------------------ |
| 🌑 **Nothing**           | 36%         | Empty-handed return.                 |
| 💎 **Deposit**           | 29%         | Iron / Silica / NovaGas.             |
| 🚀 **Wreck**             | 10%         | Abandoned ships recovered.           |
| ⚔️ **Hostile encounter** | 8%          | Combat against pirates.              |
| 🌀 **Anomaly**           | 6%          | Delayed (or advanced) return.        |
| 👽 **Alien encounter**   | 5%          | Hard combat (more advanced enemy).   |
| ✨ **Solar (SLR)**        | 4%          | Solar token.                         |
| ☄️ **Meteorite**         | 1%          | Raw material for **asteroids**.      |
| 🕳️ **Black hole**       | 1%          | **Total loss of the fleet.**         |

### 💎 Deposit (resources)

The most lucrative outcome. The **sum of resources found** equals the **discoverable total** (the cap described below). The **type** of find is then drawn:

| Draw          | Chance | Composition                                          |
| ------------- | ------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Iron only** | 50%    | The whole total in Iron.                             |
| **Silica only** | 20%  | The whole total in Silica.                           |
| **NovaGas only** | 10% | The whole total in NovaGas.                          |
| **All three** | 20%    | Split **Iron : Iron/2 : Iron/3** (ratio 6 : 3 : 2).  |

Landing on **NovaGas only** is thus a "good draw", the rarest resource, for the same value.

The loot **cap** follows a **tier** logic: it is the minimum between the **integrity of the fleet sent** (the bigger the fleet, the higher the potential) and a **tier indexed on the average of the server's top 3 players**, all **multiplied by the universe speed**. The **scale** of the discovery then varies widely from one expedition to another: most finds are **modest**, some **large**, and a few rare expeditions bring back an **exceptional** discovery near the cap.

> 💡 **Note**
>
> The fleet counts **twice**: its **integrity** raises the potential cap, and its **cargo hold** determines what can actually be brought back. A war fleet with no cargo ships will have its deposit clipped for lack of transport capacity, the optimum mixes power and hold.

### 🚀 Wreck

The fleet recovers **abandoned ships**. Their value follows the **same cap as deposits** (tiers indexed on the server top average × universe speed, bounded by the **integrity of the fleet sent**), taken at **half** of that cap: as a find, a ship is worth the equivalent of half a deposit. As with deposits, the scale varies, most often modest.

> ⚓ **Key rule: you never find bigger than what you send.**
>
> The type recovered is drawn only among ships whose **integrity** is **less than or equal** to the **largest ship of the fleet sent**. Send small, find only small; to hope to recover a heavy ship, you must commit one of comparable size. Also **excluded** from any recovery are the **Astral Titan**, the **Genesis Spaceship** and the **Scrapper**.

The value budget is then filled by the drawn type: a **light** ship gives many, a **big** one few.

### ✨ Solar (SLR)

A discovery of Solar (SLR), the game's token, drawn from a PvE pool fed by the game economy (a share of Solar spending is paid into it). Each find pays out a fraction of the pool (on the order of ~0.25%), within a daily cap (~1% of the pool) to avoid unlimited farming. As a result, the fuller the pool, the more generous the gains.

### ☄️ Meteorite

A **meteorite** drifts in the void and the fleet brings it back. It is the **raw material of asteroids**: **20 meteorites** fuse into **1 asteroid**, the first link of the rarity system (see [5.20 Rarities & Asteroids](5.20-raretes-and-asteroides.md)). Deep Space is not the only source, **destroying a moon with the Astral Titan** also releases several, but the expedition remains a steady trickle for anyone aiming to climb in rarity.

### ⚔️ Hostile & 👽 alien encounter

An enemy fleet appears and combat begins at once. Its **size adapts to the fleet you send**, and the enemy fights with **your own research level, shifted by one tier**:

* **Hostile (pirates)**: research = **yours − 3** (floor 0), and power generally **lower** than yours: winnable most of the time, without being a mere formality.
* **Alien**: research = **yours + 3**: the enemy is **more advanced than you** and its fleet can **exceed yours** in power. A real danger.

The combat is real (rounds, losses on both sides) and destroyed ships leave a **debris field** recyclable at `[.. : 16]`.

> ⚔️ **House advantage: Nephilim**
>
> The enemy's research tier (**−3** for pirates, **+3** for aliens) is computed on your **base** combat research. House bonuses apply only **afterward**, to your fleet alone: a **Nephilim** therefore enters combat with its **+2 effective levels** of combat research _on top_ of this calculation. The gap narrows in its favor, it faces an alien only **+1** above it (instead of +3) and crushes pirates at **−5**. Deep Space thus rewards the Nephilim's military calling. _(Conversely, the **Nephilim Rebuke** fights at −1: the gap widens against it.)_

> ⚠️ **Warning**
>
> **Winning a fight offers no loot.** A hostile encounter is a **risk**, not a source of gains: you come out with your fleet (minus its losses), nothing more. The expedition's rewards come from the other outcomes (Deposit, Wreck, Solar).

### 🌀 Space-time anomaly

The fleet is caught by a distortion: its **return is shifted** by a fraction of the holding duration, **most often delayed**, sometimes advanced.

> ⚠️ **Warning**
>
> The anomaly **yields no resources**: it is an event you **endure**, not a find, same logic as hostile encounters.

### 🕳️ Black hole

The fatal, rare outcome: **the entire fleet is lost**, with no loot. The accepted risk of deep exploration.

### ♻️ Zone wealth (universal depletion)

Each system has its own **Deep Space wealth**, a reservoir **shared by all players**. Each expedition resolved there **depletes the zone by −5%**, no matter who launches it: two empires farming `[1 : 1 : 16]` drain it **together**. Conversely, a zone left alone **regenerates by +4% per hour** (full in ~25 h). Wealth never drops below a **floor of 20%**.

An expedition's **yield** (**resources** and **ships** found) is directly **multiplied by the zone's current wealth**, while combat is not softened. **Solar** is the exception: its gain depends on the **PvE pool**, not on zone wealth.

A **color indicator** signals each zone's state, in the Atlas (dot) as well as when dispatching a fleet:

| Band | Wealth | State |
| ----- | -------- | ---- |
| 🟢 **Rich**     | 80 – 100% | Nearly untouched zone, full yield. |
| 🟡 **Dented**   | 50 – 79%  | Already exploited, decent yield.   |
| 🟠 **Poor**     | 30 – 49%  | Well mined, reduced gains.         |
| 🔴 **Depleted** | < 30%     | At the floor (20%), let it breathe. |

> 💡 **Note**
>
> The optimal strategy is **rotation between systems**: a fresh field yields five times more than a depleted one. Since wealth is **shared**, a very busy zone stays persistently low, seeking out the **green** systems, away from the crowds, is an advantage in itself.
