# 5.5 Fleets & Combat

The Solaris combat system is **asynchronous and deterministic**. The player defines a mission (target, speed, composition) and the result is computed automatically by the resolution engine upon arrival.

### Combat resolution

The steps are:

1. **Fleet arrival**
2. **Shot calculation**: Each round, every ship fires at a random enemy target. Damage depends on the Attack and Shield stats.
3. **Rapid Fire (RF) application**: Some ship types gain extra shots against specific target types, creating **strategic counters**.
4. **Victory conditions**: Combat lasts until one side is wiped out or after a maximum of **6 rounds**. The attacker wins if ships survive and can carry away resources.
5. **Debris field**: **30% of the value** (Iron + Silica) of destroyed ships forms a debris field recoverable by Scrappers.

> 💡 **Note**
>
> **Moon formation**: Each debris field has a **1% to 20%** chance of generating a moon, proportional to the total value of destroyed ships.

## ⏸️ The Orbiter: stationing and relaunching from a layover

Rather than always making the round trip from home, a fleet can **make a layover** at an ally's and set off again from there.

* **Stationing at an ally's**: you send your fleet on **layover** on an allied planet or moon, where it stays safe for as long as you like. Handy for projecting power far from your own worlds or supporting a front.
* **Relaunch from the layover**: a fleet on layover **sets off directly from the host planet**, as if it were a forward base, instead of having to return home first. Flight times are computed **from the layover**.
* **Multi-layover**: you can chain layovers step by step to **advance a fleet across the galaxy** stage by stage.

> 💡 **Note**
>
> The layover is a **temporary, cooperative safe harbor**: the fleet stays yours, the host cannot use it. It is a tool for **logistics and projection**, not a transfer of ownership.
