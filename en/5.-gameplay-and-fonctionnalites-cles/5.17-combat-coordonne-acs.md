# 5.17 Coordinated Combat (ACS)

**ACS** (*Alliance Combat System*) lets several members of the same alliance **fight together**: in attack as well as in defense. It is the peak of tactical play in Solaris: fleets from several players striking **to the second** in a single battle, or standing guard on an allied planet to protect it.

> ℹ️ **Status**
>
> ACS is **reserved for alliance war** (see below) and deployed server-side alongside the [5.16 Alliance War](5.16-guerre-dalliance.md) system.

> 🔒 **War-only**
>
> **Coordinated combat is only available between alliances in a declared war.** Outside war, only classic solo attacks exist. The vision: *coordinated combat is an act of war*. Concretely, the "Group attack" option only appears on a target if your alliance is **at war** with theirs, and a defensive garrison can only be deployed if your alliance is waging **at least one active war**.

---

## ⚔️ Group attack (Union)

Several allied fleets arrive **together** on the same target and fight as **one aggregated fleet**: far more powerful than the sum of isolated attacks, because they strike in a single assault instead of being repelled one by one.

### Forming a union

1. **The leader** launches their fleet toward the target normally. Their **arrival time becomes the rendezvous point `T`** of the union.
2. **The allies join** the union before registration closes (just before `T`). The server **automatically slows** each member fleet, computing its speed as tightly as possible, so it lands **exactly at `T`**. A fleet too far or too slow to arrive on time is **refused**.
3. On arrival, **a single group combat** resolves: all allied ships form the attacking side.

### Union rules

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| **Max participants** | **8 players** per union |
| **Range** | Members of the **same alliance** |
| **Target** | An **enemy planet or moon** (a union targets only one body) |
| **Fleet slots** | Each committed fleet counts in the fleet slots of **its** owner |

> 💡 **Note: several fleets, one participant**
>
> A single player can commit **several of their fleets** to a union. They then **merge** into one fleet (whose speed aligns on the slowest), which counts as **1 player** in the union and **1 participant** in combat. The number of fleets a player can have in flight remains governed by the **Computer** (`1 + Computer Technology level`).

### Research, loot & anti-bashing

* **Research per participant**: each fleet fights with **its owner's own military research** (weapons, shields, armor). A union does not homogenize technologies: each player brings their real level.
* **Pro-rata loot**: plunder (up to 50% of the target's resources) is **split among attackers pro rata to each one's surviving cargo capacity**, credited when their fleet returns. No share is lost: everyone can always carry their own.
* **Anti-bashing per fleet**: each participant **individually** faces the protections at launch. An entire union counts as only **one attack per player** against the aggressiveness limit, impossible to bypass the attack cap by coordinating. If the target is protected (shield, vacation…), **all join attempts fail**.

### Leaving a union

As long as the fleet has not arrived, a participant can **recall** it: it leaves the union and becomes a classic solo attack again. If the **leader** withdraws, the union **continues** with the remaining members. If the target **disappears** before arrival (decolonized, moon destroyed), **all fleets turn back** to their origin.

## 🛡️ Group defense (Garrison)

The mirror of attack: sending a fleet to **station on an allied planet** to protect it. The fleet **stands guard** until a chosen deadline, then **leaves automatically** back to its owner. If the guarded planet is **attacked during the guard**, the garrison **joins the defense**.

> ✅ **Good to know: it is not a gift of ships**
>
> Unlike permanent deployment ("station"), a defensive garrison **remains the defender's property**: its ships are never transferred, the fleet is **recallable** at any time, and it returns on its own at the end of the guard. In combat, it fights with **its own owner's research**.

### Deploying a garrison

* **Target**: the planet (or moon) of a **member of your alliance** (not your own: to guard at home, you simply keep your fleet docked).
* **Guard duration**: chosen among tiers: **1 / 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 / 32 h**.
* **NovaGas cost**: the round-trip flight **and** the **guard upkeep** are **prepaid on dispatch**, in NovaGas. The total cost is **shown explicitly** before confirming ("guarding X h costs Y NovaGas") and **recomputed** at each duration change. Insufficient NovaGas → dispatch refused. The guard is **non-refundable** (even on early recall).
* **No hostility**: deploying a garrison consumes no aggressiveness and triggers no anti-bashing protection.

### During the guard

* The fleet appears in the **"On guard"** phase with a countdown and a **Recall** button.
* At the deadline (or on recall), it leaves in the **return** phase to its owner.
* The owner of the defended planet sees an **"Allied garrisons"** panel (who is guarding, which fleet, until when).

### In case of attack

If an enemy attacks the guarded planet, **all garrisons present join the defense** alongside the owner's fleet and **fixed defenses**:

* **Losses are applied to each participant separately** (each takes hits with their own research).
* A **surviving** garrison **stays on guard**; an **annihilated** garrison disappears.
* The **combat report** presents an **"Allied reinforcements"** block detailing the losses and survivors of each defender.

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| **Max defenders per body** | **8 players** |
| **Range** | Members of the **same alliance** |
| **Defense of a moon** | Allowed (same mechanic as a planet) |

> ⚠️ **Warning: forced withdrawal**
>
> If a defender **leaves or is kicked** from their alliance while their garrison stands guard, it **leaves immediately**: no "ghost" allied fleet stationed at a non-member's.

## 📡 Visibility & detection

* **Espionage reveals garrisons**: a spy report on a defended planet **lists the guarding fleets** as defender fleets (depending on the **defender's** scanner level). An attacker can therefore **spot a coordinated defense before engaging**.
* **Incoming group attack = aggregated display**: the fleets of a single union arriving together are grouped on the defender's side into **a single "Group attack (n fleets)" entry**, and **a single** alert banner. The **Sensor Phalanx** lets you detail the composition, fleet by fleet.

---

## 🧬 The combat engine

ACS relies on the **same ship-by-ship combat engine** as classic PvP (see [5.5 Fleets & Combat](5.5-flottes-and-combat.md)): successive rounds, random targeting, probabilistic rapid fire, shield bounce, explosions. The only difference is that **each ship is tagged by its owner**, in order to apply the right research and **credit each destruction to the right player**.

> ✅ **Good to know**
>
> An 8-vs-8 fight costs **about as much** as a 1-vs-1 with the same total count: it is the **total number of ships** that matters, not the number of participants. The engine is optimized to stay smooth even on very large battles.

---

## 📊 Combat report

Each ACS battle generates **a readable report for every participant**:

* on the attacking side, an **"Allied reinforcements"** panel lists the committed fleets, their losses and their share of loot;
* on the defending side, the same block details the home fleet **and** each allied garrison;
* the **war score** is updated by **exact per-shot attribution** (see [5.16](5.16-guerre-dalliance.md)): each player only scores what they **actually destroyed**.

## 💰 War compensation

Fighting in ACS has a cost, and Solaris **reimburses that cost on the battlefield**. Each ACS combat turns the debris into a **War DF**: a special debris field, **recyclable only by the two belligerent alliances**. When an ally recycles it, they keep **15%** in their hold and pay the remaining **85%** to their side's **war treasury**.

This treasury **reimburses the participants' fleet losses**, pro rata to what each one lost, in Iron and Silica. The compensation is **claimable at any time**, credited to the home planet, and anything unclaimed is **paid automatically at the end of the war**. Once all the side's losses are covered, the remaining **surplus** is shared at closing **pro rata to engagement** (the tonnage actually sent into combat). A message from **Solaris Command** sums up to each commander what they received.

> ℹ️ **Struck on the field, not transferred**
>
> The compensation comes **from the debris**, never from another player's stock: it therefore **does not conflict with anti-push/pull**. In return, the war DF is **contested**: the side that controls the field after the battle and brings its recyclers scoops the pot. A **per-war detail** screen shows, for each side, the treasury and the compensation of each member (loss, covered, claimed).

---

> 🎯 **In short**: ACS turns alliance war into a real team game: synchronizing eight fleets for a devastating assault, or locking down a key planet under a wall of allied garrisons. Two faces of the same weapon, reserved for those who dared to **declare war**.
